Research

There are already some technologies that attempt to support individuals with dementia, but many are still in early stages or have important limitations.

Some prototypes have been developed, but they are not widely available for public use or purchase. This makes them inaccessible to the majority of people who could benefit from them.
One emerging solution is wearable technology, such as smart glasses with facial recognition and AI assistance. For example, CrossSense is developing smart glasses that can recognize faces, guide users through tasks, and provide real-time reminders to support daily life.
In addition, other wearable devices—like smartwatches and tracking systems—focus on monitoring health, location, or safety. These tools can track movement, send alerts, and help caregivers, but they often do not directly address memory or recognition challenges.
Organizations such as CareYaya highlight how current technology mainly focuses on reminders, monitoring, or safety rather than meaningful interaction or emotional connection.

Limitations of Existing Solutions

  • Many products are not yet available or fully developed
  • Devices often focus only on tracking or reminders, not real-time understanding
  • There are concerns about privacy and data security
  • Many solutions do not support emotional connection or relationships

How Our Product Improves on Existing Solutions

  • Focuses on real-time assistance and recognition, not just tracking
  • Prioritizes human connection and relationships, not just safety
  • Reduces complexity while still using advanced technology
